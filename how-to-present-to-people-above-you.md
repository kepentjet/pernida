# Presenting to Senior Leaders (AAA)

Source: Soph Hirst, "How to present to senior leaders (without losing them in the first minute)", Workbaby, Sep 2026. https://www.workbaby.com/p/execs-dont-hate-detail-they-hate

## Three things to know

1. Your topic is one of many on their plate. They give it a small fraction of their attention.
2. Execs want the right detail. Open at their level, then bring them into the evidence.
3. Flip your usual structure. Put "why it matters" and "what I need" first, then the support.

## The AAA check

Start with Action. It's the part you control most. The three feed each other, so revisit them as you go.

Action
- What should be true when the meeting ends?
- What do I need from this person: a decision, a resource, a sign-off, or awareness?

Audience
- Who is it? What do they own?
- What are they measured on, or what do they keep bringing up? Which of those connects to my topic?
- How much do they know about this area? What's the least context they need?
- What are their usual questions, pushbacks, or pet topics?
- What's their style: slides or docs, pre-reads, how much data, whose view they always ask for?
- Who presents to them often? Ask that person how the plan will land and what you'll get asked.

Altitude
- What's the one thing to land? Say it in 30 seconds.
- If they ask "so what?", what's my answer?
- What are my 2-3 strongest supporting points, and the best evidence for each?
- What detail goes in the appendix?
- If the time gets cut to 5 minutes, what stays?

## Keep a dossier on each exec

The Audience questions get easier every time you meet the same exec. Keep one file per exec with their priorities and KPIs, how they like information, likes and dislikes, what they keep saying, and what others say about them (note who said it). Add to it after each meeting. Keep it professional.

## Structure

1. The point, tied to their priority
2. The ask
3. 2-3 supporting points with evidence
4. Show what else you have and let them choose to go deeper

## Example from the article

A junior marketer had 15 minutes with the CMO. She opened with SMB revenue, which the CMO cared about most, then said where the talk was going. She gave three points and played a customer call recording as proof. The detailed diagnostic map stayed available in case the CMO wanted it.

## Remember

Altitude goes both ways. Open high and go deep when asked. You know the ground-level facts better than they do.
